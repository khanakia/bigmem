Storage reference · every drive type explained

Every type of storage & drive — HDD, SSD, NVMe, decoded.

The whole storage family: spinning HDDs vs flash SSDs, the SATA vs NVMe split, M.2 form factors, the PCIe Gen3/4/5 speed tiers, NAND flash types (TLC/QLC), endurance (TBW), a launch-year timeline, rough prices per TB, and a picker that tells you exactly what to buy for a dev box or video editing.

HDD
Spinning magnetic disk. Slow, cheap, huge capacity. Bulk storage & backup.
SATA SSD
Flash on the old SATA connector. ~10× faster than HDD, but capped at ~550 MB/s.
NVMe SSD (M.2)
Flash on the PCIe bus. The fast modern drive — 7,000 MB/s+. What you boot from.
Optane / other
Persistent memory, external, optical — niche or legacy. Know they exist.
Clear up the #1 confusion — "SSD or NVMe?" is not either/or. NVMe is a kind of SSD. Storage splits into HDD (spinning disk) and SSD (flash, no moving parts). SSDs then split by connector: SATA SSD (old, ~550 MB/s) vs NVMe SSD (PCIe, ~7,000 MB/s). So an NVMe drive is an SSD — just the fast kind. The full spec of the one you want reads: "2 TB M.2 NVMe Gen4 SSD" → SSD (flash) · M.2 (shape) · NVMe (fast protocol) · Gen4 (speed tier).

Which storage do I buy?

Pick your use — it tells you the exact drive. For your machine you'll want two: a fast NVMe for the OS/work, and optionally a big cheap drive for backup.

The 30-second rule: buy a 2 TB M.2 NVMe Gen4 SSD, TLC, with DRAM for your OS + work — one purchase covers 95% of people. Add a second NVMe for VM/video scratch, and a big cheap HDD only if you need bulk backup. Skip SATA SSDs (obsolete), Gen5 (hot, pricey, no real-world gain for you), and QLC for anything you write to a lot.

Endurance & the terms that scare people

  • TBW (Terabytes Written) = how much total data you can write before the warranty ends. A 2 TB TLC drive is typically 600–1,200 TBW. Even writing 100 GB/day, that's 15+ years — so for normal dev/editing you will almost never wear one out. Heavy VM churn or 4K video capture eats it faster, so size up (bigger drive = more TBW).
  • DWPD (Drive Writes Per Day) = enterprise version of the same idea — how many times you can rewrite the whole drive daily for the warranty period. Consumer drives don't advertise it; ignore unless buying server SSDs.
  • DRAM vs DRAM-less: a work drive should have a DRAM cache — it stays fast during big/sustained writes (compiling, copying VMs). DRAM-less drives are cheaper and fine for a boot/media drive but stutter under heavy load.
  • SLC cache (pseudo-cache): most TLC/QLC drives run a fast SLC-mode buffer for bursts. When it fills on a huge write, speed drops to the drive's "native" rate — much lower on QLC. Another reason to prefer TLC for heavy work.
  • Heat / heatsink: Gen4 is fine bare or with the board's built-in heatsink. Gen5 runs hot and often needs an active heatsink — one more reason Gen4 is the practical pick.

So which is fastest — and what should YOU buy?

Speed ranking: NVMe Gen5 (~12–14 GB/s) > NVMe Gen4 (~7 GB/s) > NVMe Gen3 (~3.5 GB/s) > SATA SSD (~0.55 GB/s) > HDD (~0.2 GB/s). But fastest ≠ best for you — beyond Gen4, real-world app/boot times barely change; you'd only feel Gen5 moving giant files all day. Your setup: a 2 TB Gen4 TLC NVMe for OS + Docker, a second 2–4 TB Gen4 NVMe for VM/video scratch, and — only if you archive lots of footage — a big cheap HDD for backup. That combo beats one giant expensive drive.
Note on the 395 mini-PC: most 395 boxes have 1–2 M.2 NVMe slots you can upgrade (unlike the soldered RAM). So even on the sealed mini-PC, storage is the one part you can add to — check the model has a free M.2 slot before buying. On your desktop build, storage is fully open: add drives anytime.
